# Qualification of the AI tasks with real providers

Evidence for M4-A9 and P7-A2 of [the completion plan](../plans/m4-completion.md): fixtures of
classification, extraction, translation, reply drafting and the work of a managed agent, run through
the same boundary as the product (`runAiTask`, the intake, the translation job, the draft assistant
and the runner) against the live APIs of four providers (three kinds of protocol). The script is
[`scripts/qualify-ai.ts`](../../scripts/qualify-ai.ts); it needs the private development stack, makes a
project of its own and removes it, reads the key from the environment and does not print or store it
(the result files were checked for the key). The data is made up. The checks are programmatic and
deliberately plain (a verdict, text that is made of the lines of the mail, a number or a link that
survives, no invented date, no refund that a message to the customer confirms); they do not replace
reading the results, which are in the tables.

```bash
QUALIFY_KIND=anthropic QUALIFY_MODEL=claude-haiku-5-5 QUALIFY_KEY=... pnpm exec tsx scripts/qualify-ai.ts out.md
QUALIFY_KIND=openai QUALIFY_MODEL=gpt-4.1 QUALIFY_KEY=... pnpm exec tsx scripts/qualify-ai.ts out.md
# a gateway that speaks the protocol of OpenAI, with a wait that fits its limits per minute
QUALIFY_KIND=openai QUALIFY_MODEL=qwen3-30b-a3b-instruct-2507 QUALIFY_KEY=... \
  QUALIFY_ENDPOINT=https://chat-ai.academiccloud.de QUALIFY_PAUSE_MS=9000 pnpm exec tsx scripts/qualify-ai.ts out.md
QUALIFY_KIND=openai QUALIFY_MODEL=openai/gpt-oss-120b QUALIFY_KEY=... \
  QUALIFY_ENDPOINT=https://api.groq.com/openai QUALIFY_PAUSE_MS=20000 pnpm exec tsx scripts/qualify-ai.ts out.md
```

Dates of the runs: 2026-10-15 (Anthropic, OpenAI) and 2026-10-16 (GWDG, Groq); the first two on the revision of the pull request that added this file (the stack of
intake, translation, knowledge and issues on top of the merged runner and stops).

## Google (gemini-3.5-flash)

Not qualified. The key in use is a free tier with a daily quota; the run ended with
`ai_quota_exhausted` on every call (0 of 20 fixtures), so nothing can be said about the quality of
Gemini for these tasks. What the run does show is the behaviour on failure: every mail still became
a ticket, marked as not cleared by the classification (`junkCheck: failed`) or kept as the parser
read it with the failure on the message (`extractionFailure`), translations ended `failed` with the
code, and the runner ended `failed` without a reply. The fixtures are to be run again with a key that
has quota.

## What the open-weight models showed

- **A model that thinks needs a larger output cap.** `qwen3.5-397b-a17b` spent more than a thousand output tokens on a
  verdict of one word, because its reasoning counts as output. With the cap of 4000 output tokens that the profiles of these runs have, two
  translations ended as `truncated` (the product refuses a cut-off translation and says so), and a third
  translation and two drafts failed (`translation_unusable`, `ai_provider_error`; the cause of the two refusals of
  the provider was not diagnosed). It passed 15 of 20. `gpt-oss-120b` at Groq also reasons and passed 20 of 20 with the same
  cap, so this is a property of a model and of its cap, not of the gateway.
- **An instruct model without reasoning is faster and failed on instructions.** `qwen3-30b-a3b-instruct-2507` passed
  18 of 20. It gave no usable extraction for the mail with an instruction inside (the parser result stayed, as designed)
  and wrote a draft with the placeholders `[shipping date]` and `[order status]` that did not name the
  knowledge source, so the check failed. Nothing was sent in either case.
- **Limits of the providers shaped the runs.** The Chat AI of the GWDG answered with 30 requests a minute, 200 an
  hour, 1000 a day and 3000 a month for the key that was used; Groq's free tier showed 1000 requests and 8000 tokens a
  minute. The runs wait between fixtures (9 and 20 seconds).

## What the runs do not show

- Each fixture ran once per provider; the results of a model can differ between runs, and a pass is a
  sample, not a guarantee. Small models follow injected instructions more often than the models used
  here; the settings page says so.
- Negation, intent and terms of a translation are checked by two fixtures and by reading, not by rule.
- The provider cost of the runs is in the usage column; it is small.
- The worker of the development stack can take an incoming mail before the script does; the result
  is read from the stored ticket either way.
